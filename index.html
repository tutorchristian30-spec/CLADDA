Gallery 02 · Foundations

Understanding Media and Information

Three connected ideas help us understand how messages are created, shared, and evaluated.

📺

What is Media?

Media refers to the different channels, platforms, and tools used to create, communicate, and distribute messages to an audience. Examples include newspapers, television, radio, websites, and social media.

💡

What is Information?

Information refers to facts, ideas, data, and knowledge that help people understand situations, make decisions, and develop awareness about the world.

🧠

What is Media Literacy?

Media literacy is the ability to access, analyze, evaluate, create, and communicate information through different forms of media. It allows individuals to become critical thinkers who can distinguish reliable information from misleading content.

Gallery 03 · The media collection

The Different Forms of Media

Explore four major categories, their examples, advantages, and limitations.

📰📚
A · Print media

Printed materials

Print media refers to information distributed through printed materials.

Examples

  • Newspapers
  • Books
  • Magazines
  • Brochures
  • Flyers

Advantages

  • Provides detailed information.
  • Can be kept and read repeatedly.
  • Does not require an internet connection.

Disadvantages

  • Requires printing materials and resources.
  • Distribution may take time.
  • Information may not be updated immediately.
📻📺
B · Broadcast media

Audio and audiovisual channels

Broadcast media delivers information through audio and audiovisual channels.

Examples

  • Radio
  • Television

Advantages

  • Reaches a wide audience.
  • Provides timely news and announcements.
  • Combines sound and visuals to communicate information.

Disadvantages

  • Usually follows scheduled programming.
  • Audiences may have limited control over content.
  • Production and broadcasting can be expensive.
💻🌐
C · Digital media

Electronic content

Digital media refers to content created, stored, and distributed electronically.

Examples

  • Websites
  • Online newspapers
  • Social media
  • Podcasts
  • Streaming platforms

Advantages

  • Allows fast communication.
  • Provides easy access to information.
  • Encourages interaction and participation.
  • Makes sharing content convenient.

Disadvantages

  • Can spread misinformation quickly.
  • May expose users to privacy risks.
  • Can cause excessive screen time and distraction.
🤖✨
D · Emerging media

Artificial intelligence

Artificial intelligence, or AI, refers to technology that can perform tasks commonly associated with human intelligence, such as generating content, recognizing patterns, and processing language.

Examples

  • AI chatbots
  • AI image generators
  • Automated recommendations
  • Virtual assistants

AI can support education, research, and creativity. However, users must verify its outputs because AI-generated information can contain errors, bias, or misleading details.

Gallery 05 · Society and media

The Influence of Media on Society

Media affects people's thoughts, behaviors, and decisions. Its impact can be helpful or harmful.

🌱

Positive Influences

  1. Education – Provides learning resources and educational opportunities.
  2. Communication – Connects people regardless of distance.
  3. Awareness – Spreads information about social issues and current events.
  4. Entertainment – Offers various forms of recreation and creative expression.
  5. Cultural Preservation – Helps promote traditions, languages, and cultural practices.
⚠️

Negative Influences

  1. Misinformation – Spreads false or inaccurate information.
  2. Cyberbullying – Can be used to harass or harm others online.
  3. Addiction and Distraction – Excessive media consumption may affect productivity.
  4. Privacy Risks – Personal information may be exposed or misused.
  5. Unrealistic Expectations – Some online content promotes unhealthy comparisons.
Key takeaway: Media can create positive changes, but its effects depend on how people create, share, and consume information.
Gallery 06 · Verification lab

Misinformation – Pause Before You Share

Not every post or headline online is accurate. Learn the difference and practice checking before sharing.

❔

Misinformation

False or inaccurate information shared regardless of whether the person sharing it intends to deceive.

🎭

Disinformation

False information deliberately created or shared to mislead others.

🔍 How to Identify Misleading Information

Check the Source

Determine who published the information and whether the source is trustworthy.

Verify the Evidence

Look for facts, supporting documents, and credible references.

Examine the Date

Make sure the information is current and relevant.

Compare Other Sources

Check whether other reliable organizations report the same information.

Investigate Images

Determine whether photographs or videos have been edited or taken out of context.

“Do not believe everything you see online. Always verify information before accepting or sharing it.”

Responsible Digital Citizenship

Being a responsible digital citizen means using technology and online platforms safely, ethically, and respectfully.

🔐

Protect privacy

Protect personal information and privacy. Use strong passwords.

🤝

Show respect

Respect other people's opinions. Avoid cyberbullying and harmful online behavior.

©️

Give credit

Give proper credit to original creators.

🧭

Think first

Verify information before sharing. Think carefully before posting online.

“Our actions online reflect our responsibilities as members of the digital community.”

Gallery 09 · Personal reflection

What We Learned Beyond the Screen

“

Media and information have become important parts of modern society. Through this activity, we realized that media is not simply a tool for entertainment and communication but also a powerful source of knowledge that can influence people's thoughts, decisions, and actions.

We learned that being connected to the internet does not automatically mean being well-informed. We must develop the ability to evaluate information, recognize misleading content, and understand the possible effects of what we share online.

As students and media users, we have a responsibility to become more critical, careful, and respectful when using different media platforms. Ultimately, technology becomes more meaningful when we use it with knowledge, awareness, and responsibility.
